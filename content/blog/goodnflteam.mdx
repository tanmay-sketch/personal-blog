The NFL is interesting because there's many nuances and factors within the sport that we don't fully understand as non players but I understand stats and I'm looking for patterns

I was recently reading <a href="https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691177625/mathletics?srsltid=AYCX9vK4HRchmpBMWH3MMGoALfeXYSItwBo_rM-ellYae27HUGG_ryEY" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Mathletics</a> 
and came across the chapter "What makes NFL teams win?". It was quite interesting so I decided to do my own analysis and find out indicators
of a winning NFL team. 

In the early 1960s Bud Goode (sports statistician) studied the exact same question of what makes a team win. He found that **yards per pass attempt**
in both offense and defense is one of the most important factors. So I decided to look at yards per attempt in rushing and passing both offensively and defensively
to see if that had any impact on the scoring margin of a team.

<Callout type="default">All the code for this analysis can be found <a href="https://github.com/tanmay-sketch/blog_code/blob/main/goodnflteam/notebook.ipynb" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">here</a></Callout>

The data is coming from **nflverse's** python package <a href="https://github.com/nflverse/nflreadpy"  target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">nflreadpy</a>

We are only looking at the regular season games, these columns from the dataset will be used to build our features for two models we'll be using to predict
the scoring margin. **Linear Regression** and **XGBoost**.


Passing gives us **NPYA** on offense and **NDPYA** on defense; rushing gives us **NRYA** and **NDRYA**.
Both calculations use regular-season totals. For offense, we use the team's own numbers; for defense, we use its opponents' numbers from those same games.

**Passing / Defense**

<Callout type="default">
    <MathBlock>{"\\frac{\\text{Passing yards} + \\text{Sack yardage}}{\\text{Pass attempts} + \\text{Sacks taken}}"}</MathBlock>
</Callout>

**Rushing / Defense**

<Callout type="default">
    <MathBlock>{"\\frac{\\text{Rushing yards}}{\\text{Carries}}"}</MathBlock>
</Callout>

Sack yardage keeps the sign recorded in `sack_yards_lost` when added to passing yards.

For this analysis I decided to look at 3 different time frames of data in predicting the scoring margin. Data from
**2015-2024**, **2020-2024**, **2022-2024**. This is to see if our features change over different time periods or whether they
remain consistent.

The features themselves don't have much correlation among each other. **NPYA** and scoring margin have a positive correlation while **NDPYA**
and scoring margin have a negative correlation. Its not strong but we expect these numbers to be the case because the teams that tend to pass
better have higher scoring margins while the teams that allow teams to pass more efficiently reduce the scoring margin.

The scoring margin (target) is each team's **average scoring margin per game** over the regular season: points scored minus points allowed, averaged across its games.

These models are evaluated on the held-out 2025 season data, using each team's 2025 passing and rushing efficiency to estimate its average scoring margin per game.

For the **2015–2024 model**, let <Math>{"\\widehat{M}"}</Math> be the predicted average scoring margin in points per game. Using rounded coefficients:

<Callout type="default">
    <MathBlock>{"\\widehat{M} = 4.89\\,\\mathrm{NPYA} - 5.01\\,\\mathrm{NDPYA} + 1.47\\,\\mathrm{NRYA} - 1.22\\,\\mathrm{NDRYA}"}</MathBlock>
</Callout>

All four coefficients are statistically significant at the 5% level (<Math>{"p < 0.05"}</Math>).

Holding the other three features constant, the coefficients have the following interpretations:

- **Passing offense:** one additional net yard per passing attempt is associated with a **4.89 point higher predicted scoring margin per game**.
- **Passing defense:** allowing one fewer net yard per passing attempt is associated with a **5.01 point higher predicted scoring margin per game**.
- **Rushing offense:** one additional yard per carry is associated with a **1.47 point higher predicted scoring margin per game**.
- **Rushing defense:** allowing one fewer yard per carry is associated with a **1.22 point higher predicted scoring margin per game**.

We also observe that Passing yards per attempt have a significantly higher difference in scoring margin compared to rushing yards per attempt. 

The negative defensive coefficients mean allowing more passing or rushing yards per attempt reduce the scoring margin. These coefficients make sense 
and remain consistent to Goode's findings.

The coefficients remain similar however the model trained using data from **2022-2024** to predict scoring margin shows a drop in 
**NRYA**. This deserves a separate investigation to see if the smaller sample leads to this change or rushing efficiency 
is contributing lesser to scoring margin.

These results are particularly interesting. OLS <Math>{"R^2"}</Math> remains nearly constant across the three time periods for both training and test sets. 
This matches the findings in Table 1. However, the training <Math>{"R^2"}</Math> for XGBoost improves with more recent data, while its test 
<Math>{"R^2"}</Math> decreases. OLS appears to capture the meaningful signal better, while XGBoost may have overfit the data. Further tuning could improve
XGBoost's performance but the simpler model generalizes better in this situation.

To end this, I'll leave y'all with the same question I came across in <a href="https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691177625/mathletics?srsltid=AYCX9vK4HRchmpBMWH3MMGoALfeXYSItwBo_rM-ellYae27HUGG_ryEY" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Mathletics</a>.
**If it costs $10 million for an All-Pro receiver and $10 million for an All-Pro linebacker, which expenditure will improve team performance more?** Our linebacker is going to impact
**NDPYA** and **NDRYA** whereas the receiver is only going to impact **NPYA**. 

My take: **All pro receiver**. Under my model an extra net yard per passing attempt increases the scoring margin by 5 pts. Obviously its very situation dependent and doesn't tell us if 
signing the player will actually change those numbers but its good food for thought.
